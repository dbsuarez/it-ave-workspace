# Code of Conduct: Introduction & Principles of Discipline

## Introduction

**IT Avenue** has adopted a Code of Conduct that sets out the principles to guide employees in carrying out their duties and responsibilities to the highest of personal and corporate integrity. The Code outlines the standard behavior expected by the Company of all IT Avenue employees when dealing with the company, its customers, employees, business partners, competitors, and community. Operating in an ethical manner that will uphold the protection of our customers, investors, and stakeholders is essential to our success. Our customers, investors and other stakeholders rely on us to be honest, fair, and reliable. Thus, it is imperative to maintain and safeguard this reputation through ethical ways of doing business.

## Principle of Employee Discipline

Every employee, regardless of the nature of employment, must act professionally, be courteous in all their interactions, address everyone politely, treat everyone with respect, and maintain their competence for their jobs.

Every employee must not act with malice and always prioritize the interest of the Company before their own in the discharge of their functions. They must always ensure their fitness for their jobs so as not to damage the Company, their colleagues, and themselves.

Every employee must obey the law and lawful orders of the duly constituted authorities of the Philippines, observe the norms and customs of every community, and respect the different religions and culture of every person that they meet and interact with every day.

Every employee must observe discipline in the discharge of their jobs to achieve the desired result that will greatly benefit the Company without bending the rules as they may deem fit.

Every employee must observe proper decorum whenever they are in the workplace so that they will not disturb the professional atmosphere of their workplace.

# Reporting Code Breaches & Non-Retaliation Policy

## Reporting Code Breaches – Where to get help

All employees are under obligation to report any suspected or potential illegal or unethical behavior to his/her direct immediate superior or to any member of the Human Resources Department using the company Incident Report Form. The same applies when one is in doubt about the best course of action in a particular situation.

## Non-Retaliation

IT Avenue will not take any disciplinary or retaliatory action against any employee who reports in good faith. The Company will ensure that said employee is not victimized by such disclosure.

## Discipline

Violating applicable laws, regulations, or the Code, encouraging others to do so, and/or ignoring or failing to report known violations may result in disciplinary action. Discipline may include written reminder, written reprimand, employment suspension, loss of employment-related benefits or termination of employment.

# Types of Disciplinary Action

The type of disciplinary action to be imposed on an employee found guilty of an offense depends on the severity and frequency of commission of the same categorized offense. Disciplinary actions take the forms:

a) **Written Reminder.** A formal written notification issued to an employee following a policy violation or performance deficiency. It documents the nature of the infraction, the specific policy breached, and the corrective actions required to avoid further disciplinary measures, including suspension or termination.

b) **Written reprimand.** This is a letter given to an employee with a light offense warning and reprimanding him of the violations of policy, rules or regulations considered as less serious offense. It also specifies the consequences of a repetition.

c) **Suspension.** This is a disciplinary action given to more serious offenses. It deprives the employee of his pay and benefits during the period of suspension. The suspension can be at least 1 workday to 30 workdays.

d) **Dismissal for Cause.** A severe penalty given to employees for habitual, malicious or gross violation and continuous commission of offenses despite suspension. An employee found guilty of such violations is subject to termination.

# Due Process: Twin-Notice Rule

All disciplinary actions involving suspension or dismissal must strictly follow the Twin-Notice Rule:

1. First Notice (NTE): HR issues a written Notice to Explain specifying the exact act/omission and applicable policy violated. The employee is given a minimum of FIVE (5) CALENDAR DAYS to respond in writing.
2. Administrative Hearing: If the explanation is insufficient or the case warrants it, HR schedules an administrative hearing. The employee has the right to be heard and present evidence. A Notice of Administrative Hearing (NOAH) must be served at least 3 days before the scheduled hearing.
3. Second Notice (Notice of Decision): After investigation and hearing, HR issues a written decision stating the finding and penalty imposed.

> **NOTE:** Failure to observe the Twin-Notice Rule renders the disciplinary action procedurally infirm and may expose the Company to labor liability before the NLRC. This process is mandatory for ALL suspension and dismissal cases.

# Administrative Investigation Procedures & Penalty Guidelines

## Penalties for Multiple Offenses

When a single act or omission results in a violation of two or more rules; or when two or more offenses are committed simultaneously or contemporaneously (having occurred within substantially the same period) the penalty for each offense shall be imposed. Provided that in the case of dismissal and a lower penalty are to be imposed, the penalty of dismissal shall be the one imposed.

## Procedure

1. Upon receipt by the **Human Resource Department - Employee Relations Officer** of the report regarding an incident, the report will be forwarded to the line leader of the respondent or his direct superior for notification and/or additional information gathering
2. The HR-ER will then draft a Notice to Explain (NTE) which contains the incident and the proper charge/s according to this code.
3. The NTE will then be first submitted to the head of the HR department for approval.
4. The approved NTE shall then be sent to the line manager or the immediate superior of the respondent for service to the latter.
5. After receipt by the respondent of the NTE, he/she must submit a written explanation regarding the incident within five (5) calendar days from the receipt of the NTE. If the respondent refuses to receive the NTE, it shall be deemed as a waiver of his/her right to explain his/her side and the process of the resolution of the case will proceed.
6. The Human Resource Department - Employee Relations will then examine the available evidence for the case's resolution. If unsatisfied, he/she may schedule an administrative hearing regarding the case depending on the gravity of the offense. A Notice of Administrative Hearing (NOAH) will then be served to the respondent in which the latter must sign upon his/her receipt which must be made 3 days before the scheduled administrative hearing.
7. During the administrative hearing, the **HR - ER and committee** will ask searching questions to aid the investigation of the case. Any statements given by the respondent during the administrative hearing may be used against him/her. Additional offenses may also be charged against the respondent based on the statements he/she made during the administrative hearing provided that said offenses are in relation to the incident complained of.
8. A decision will then be formulated after the conclusion of the investigation in which the management will decide on whether the respondent is liable or not and on what appropriate penalty will be imposed on the respondent. Note that the Management has the prerogative to mitigate or aggravate the penalties imposed based on the attending circumstances of the case.

> **NOTE:** If the respondent is cleared from any liability, the NTE will not be reflected in his employment record.

## Provisions That Affect the Weight of Disciplinary Measure

### Mitigating Circumstances

The following are the circumstances that may mitigate the action to be taken by the Management against the erring employee:

- Contributory Negligence by the immediate head that if only had acted upon, would prevent the commission of the offense.
- Acting on an order of a Superior which is although unlawful or immoral, was ordered with abuse of authority.
- After commission of offense, the employee has voluntarily surrendered himself to the management or confessed his guilt.
- Sufficient provocation from the part of the victim.
- Any event or circumstances that are analogous to the foregoing.
- Lastly, the management reserves the right to exercise its prerogative in mitigating the offense of the erring employee when the amount is inconsequential and that the same is a necessity, depending on the circumstances.

### Aggravating Circumstances

The following circumstances are to be considered in increasing the disciplinary action against the employee:

- That the employee takes advantage of his position.
- That the employee/s commit the offense on the occasion of earthquake, epidemic or other calamity or misfortune.
- That the offense be committed with aid of outsiders.
- That the offense be committed with utter disregard on the rank of the victim.
- That the offender is a delinquent.
- That the offense be committed with evident premeditation.
- That craft, fraud or disguise be employed.
- That the offense committed has defined to be a light offense, but the damage caused to the company is incommensurate with the penalty
- When the offense is committed by an employee holding the rank of supervisory and above.
- That the resulting loss to the company is disproportionate to the disciplinary action, if strictly followed.
- Any other causes analogous to the foregoing.

# Habitual Delinquency & Cumulative Offenses

## Six (6) days suspension

Commission of any three (3) offenses with a minimum penalty of warning or suspension within a one-month period shall be penalized by six (6) days suspension.

## Twelve (12) days suspension

Commission of any four (4) offenses with a minimum penalty of warning of suspension within a one-month period shall be penalized by twelve (12) days suspension.

## Dismissal from company

Commission of any five (5) offenses with minimum penalty of warning of suspension within a one-month period shall warrant dismissal from the company.

Commission of any twelve (12) offenses with a minimum penalty of warning of suspension within a one-year period shall warrant dismissal from the company.

Commission of four (4) offenses within a three-month period punishable by suspension shall warrant dismissal from the company.

Commission of six (6) offenses within a one-year period punishable by suspension shall warrant dismissal from the company.

# Graduation of Offense

The steps of discipline, from lightest to most severe:

1. **Written Reminder** – A written notice to inform the employee of the issue and provide an opportunity to improve.
2. **Written Warning** – A formal written notice indicating that the behavior must change and future violations may result in more severe action.
3. **Suspension** – A temporary removal from work without pay due to continued misconduct or serious policy violations.
4. **Termination** – Employment is terminated due to continued misconduct, severe violations, or failure to improve.

## Graduation of Offense for Seasonal Employees

1. **Written Reminder** – A written notice to inform the employee of the issue and provide an opportunity to improve.
2. **Written Warning** – A formal written notice indicating that the behavior must change and future violations may result in more severe action.
3. **Stern Warning** – A formal notice that further violations or continued misconduct may result in suspension or termination.
4. **Termination** – Employment is terminated due to continued misconduct, severe violations, or failure to improve.

# General Policies: I. Timekeeping and Attendance Policy

## Policy Statement

The Company maintains a strict timekeeping and attendance system to ensure operational efficiency, accountability, and proper compensation of employees. Compliance with this policy is mandatory. Any violation, misuse, or manipulation of time records shall be considered a serious offense dealt with in accordance with the Code of Conduct.

## Coverage

Applies to all employees — regular, probationary, project-based, and temporary.

## Official Working Hours

Employees shall strictly observe official working hours as communicated by HR or their supervisor. Any modification of work schedules must be authorized in advance.

## Mandatory Time Recording

All employees must record their attendance daily via the official timekeeping system (biometric scanner, time card, or digital system). Required entries: Time In (start of duty) and Time Out (end of duty). Employees are given a 10-minute break grace period.

## Prohibition on Timekeeping Misconduct

The following are strictly prohibited: Buddy Punching; Falsification of time records; Tampering with the timekeeping system; Deliberate failure to record entries to conceal tardiness or undertime; Using another employee's access credentials. Violations may result in termination.

## Overtime Authorization

All overtime work must be pre-approved in writing by the employee's supervisor or department head. Unauthorized overtime may not be recognized for compensation and may constitute a policy violation.

## Correction of Timekeeping Errors

Employees must immediately report timekeeping issues to their supervisor and submit a Payroll Concern Form with written explanation. Repeated failure to record attendance properly is subject to administrative review.

## Disciplinary Measures

Violations are subject to disciplinary action consistent with the Code of Conduct, ranging from written reminder to termination depending on severity and frequency.

# General Policies: II. Policy on Company-Issued Laptops and Desktops (MEMO-2026P-018)

**Purpose:** Establishes guidelines for proper use of company-issued laptops and desktop computers to protect data security, safeguard confidential information, and ensure equipment is used solely for legitimate business purposes.

**General Guidelines:** Company property must be used strictly for work-related purposes only. Devices must be password-protected and locked when unattended. Only company-approved software may be installed.

**Prohibited Activities:** Using devices for illegal activities; sharing with unauthorized individuals; using excessive personal entertainment; copying or distributing company data without authorization.

**Return of Property:** Upon separation, termination and/or end of contract, all company property must be returned in good condition. Failure to do so may result in financial liability.

# General Policies: III. Social Media Policy (MEMO-2026P-020)

**Purpose:** Establishes guidelines for responsible social media use to protect the reputation, confidentiality, and business interests of the Company while allowing employees to responsibly engage in social media activities.

**Key Guidelines:** Employees must not post content that damages the company's reputation, discloses confidential information, or creates conflicts of interest. Company logos and trademarks must not be used without prior authorization. Excessive social media use during work hours is subject to disciplinary action.

**Violations:** May result in suspension or termination depending on the severity and nature of the offense.

# General Policies: IV. Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) Policy (MEMO-2026P-007)

**Purpose:** Ensures a safe and healthy working environment in compliance with RA 11058 (Occupational Safety and Health Standards Act) and its Implementing Rules (DO 198-18).

**OSH Committee:** The OSHC is composed of: CEO (Chairman), HR Head and Chief Compliance Officer (Vice Chairmen), one representative from each department, and a trained Safety Officer (Secretary).

**Key Responsibilities:** Conduct regular safety inspections; develop emergency response plans; promote safety awareness; investigate incidents; ensure compliance with OSH Law.

**Incident Reporting:** All accidents, injuries, fires, or unsafe conditions must be reported immediately to the line manager, OSHC, or Safety Officer. DOLE-required reports (WAIR, Annual Medical Report, AEDR) must be submitted on schedule.

# General Policies: V. Alcohol-Free Workplace Policy (MEMO-2026P-008)

IT Avenue prohibits the use, possession, solicitation, or sale of alcohol in the workplace, and any conduct that shows impairment due to alcohol. Testing types include Cause-Test, Post-Accident Test, and Random Test. Violations may result in suspension of up to twelve (12) days without pay, or termination of employment for repeat offenses.

# General Policies: VI. HIV/AIDS Workplace Policy (MEMO-2026P-009)

In compliance with RA 8504 (Philippine AIDS Prevention and Control Act). Discrimination based on HIV/AIDS status from pre-employment to post-employment is strictly prohibited. # General Policies: XI. Sexual Harassment Policy — RA 7877 & RA 11313 (Safe Spaces Act) (MEMO-2026P-014)

> **NOTE:** This policy has been updated to include coverage under RA 11313 (Safe Spaces Act of 2019), which extends anti-harassment protection to gender-based harassment in the workplace, in addition to the existing RA 7877 provisions.

IT Avenue believes that employees should work in an environment free of sexual and gender-based harassment. The Company complies with both RA 7877 (Anti-Sexual Harassment Act) and RA 11313 (Safe Spaces Act).

**Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI):** IT Avenue maintains a CODI as required under RA 11313. The CODI is composed of at least one (1) management representative, one (1) employee representative, and one (1) from any accredited organization where applicable. The CODI: receives and investigates all harassment complaints; conducts independent and confidential investigations separate from standard HR processes; issues findings and recommendations to Top Management.

**Reporting:** Any officer or employee who experiences or witnesses sexual or gender-based harassment must report immediately to the CODI or to any member of the HR Department. All allegations are investigated promptly. Identities of reporters and witnesses are protected to the extent possible.

**Non-Retaliation:** IT Avenue will permit no employment-based retaliation against anyone who reports harassment or serves as a witness in an investigation.

**Penalty:** Sexual harassment and gender-based harassment are grounds for immediate dismissal. No prior warnings are required for termination in harassment cases.

# Employee Policies: I. Absence Management Policy

## Types of Absences

**Scheduled Absences:** Planned absences (vacation leave, personal leave, offset leave, pre-approved time off).

**Unscheduled Absences:** Unplanned absences (illness, emergencies, bereavement, unforeseen circumstances).

## Leave Entitlements

**For Rank-and-File Sales Personnel:** 5 days sick leave and 5 days vacation leave per year, accruing upon regularization.

**For all other employees:** Leave entitlements are defined in the individual employment contract or applicable company policy.

## Requesting Absences

All leave requests must be filed in advance through the Company's designated leave management system. Planned absences must be requested at least two (2) weeks in advance. Failure to properly file may result in disciplinary action.

## Approval Process

Supervisors and managers review and decide on leave requests based on operational requirements. Absence of express approval is considered denial. If the supervisor does not contest the leave within two (2) days of the employee's return, it is deemed tacitly approved.

## Leave Conversion and Carry-Over

When sick leave is exhausted, vacation leave may be applied for illness. Unused vacation leave credits may be carried over for up to 15 months. Upon resignation, employees are entitled to cash conversion of unused and eligible vacation leave credits.

**Resignation Process:** Employee submits formal resignation letter to supervisor and HR. HR schedules an exit interview. All company property must be returned before the last working day. Confidentiality obligations survive separation.
